# MASTER DIRECTIVE — TRANSITION FROM SEMANTIC RECOVERY TO PRODUCTION-LIKE VALIDATION

## 1. Mission

We are changing the engineering strategy.

The objective is no longer to perform unlimited semantic-recovery iterations without delivering an operational application.

The objective is to transform the existing Fawry HR Training Chatbot into a validated, offline, production-like release candidate suitable for an authorized, isolated employee pilot.

This is NOT authorization to deploy to production.

Continue from the existing repository and preserve all completed engineering work.

Do not restart the project, replace the architecture, rebuild the knowledge base unnecessarily, or discard previous tests.

I expect a working release candidate, reproducible deployment instructions, meaningful end-to-end validation, and a precise list of remaining blockers.

## 3. Architecture and deployment constraints

Maintain the existing architecture:

- BGE-M3 embeddings.
- Chroma vector storage.
- BM25 lexical retrieval.
- Qwen local inference.
- Existing conversation runtime.
- Existing canonical knowledge and evidence contracts.
- Existing numerical and procedural validators.

Target environment:

- Windows Server.
- Offline operation.
- CPU-only.
- Maximum 16 GB RAM.
- No GPU dependency.
- No cloud inference dependency.

The system must remain operable without internet access.

Measure actual resource consumption and performance. Do not claim the system meets memory or latency requirements without native measurements.

→ language interpretation

## 7. Phase C — Production-like employee questions

Use genuine employee-style questions based on approved source content and authorized, anonymized examples where available.

Include:

- Franco-Arabic.
- Mixed Arabic and English.
- Short and incomplete questions.
- Multi-turn conversations.
- Service switching.
- Multiple services in one question.
- Comparisons.
- Fees, limits and eligibility.
- Procedures and operational steps.
- Conditions and exceptions.
- Channel restrictions.
- Quoted customer claims.
- Unsupported policies.
- Ambiguous service references.
- Questions requiring clarification.
- Questions where only part of the answer is supported.

Use a balanced evaluation design across the available catalog.

Do not fabricate approved business answers for services without verified evidence.

Separate evaluation into:

A. Interpretation-only cases that do not require business facts.

B. Evidence-grounded business-answer cases.

C. Unsupported-information and clarification cases.

D. Full native conversation cases.

E. Safety, integrity and adversarial-input cases.

Do not mix these categories into a single misleading accuracy percentage.

## 12. Definition of done

Distinguish three separate completion states.

### Engineering-ready

- Application runs end-to-end in an authorized isolated environment.
- Regression tests pass.
- Source and approval controls remain intact.
- Native measurements are recorded.
- Known critical defects are addressed.
- Deployment and rollback instructions are verified.

### Pilot-ready

- Engineering-ready conditions pass.
- Independent unseen-question evaluation meets predefined acceptance thresholds.
- Pilot knowledge has required approvals.
- Employee-facing behavior is safe and auditable.
- Pilot access and scope are explicitly authorized.

### Production-ready

- Pilot-ready conditions pass.
- Required service coverage and approvals are complete.
- Native performance and reliability requirements pass.
- Security and operational reviews are complete.
- Production deployment is explicitly approved.

Do not claim one completion state implies another.

## 13. Engineering execution policy

Work autonomously on authorized engineering tasks.

When tests fail:

1. Identify the root cause.
2. Preserve the failing evidence.
3. Correct the shared defect.
4. Add regression protection.
5. Rerun relevant tests.
6. Continue toward the release candidate.

Avoid endless cycles of narrowly targeted semantic patches.

After two unsuccessful corrections to the same architectural failure family, stop patch accumulation and produce an architecture-level comparison of alternatives.

Do not change frozen gold expectations or weaken validators to improve scores.

Do not alter production, original source documents, approval records or protected backups.

Do not request user intervention for ordinary development failures.

Request intervention only when a genuine external dependency blocks progress.
